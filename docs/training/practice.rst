4. Rehearse with a colleague
============================

Use an adult role-play partner and a training code such as ``TRAIN-001``. Follow
the study's handling rules for adult rehearsal recordings. Do not use a child's
information, credentials, or recordings as demonstration data.

This exercise checks operation and fidelity. It does not replace the study's
review of the three conditions or establish equivalence to the original RSR.

Exercise A: capture the whole interaction
-----------------------------------------

1. Sign in, configure the assigned rehearsal condition, and open the child display.
2. Start continuous recording before speaking the introduction.
3. Ask the adult to say a short phrase before any item is presented. This is a
   deliberate early-speech event for the rehearsal.
4. Deliver the lead-supplied practice procedure. In a software-only rehearsal,
   the trainer may use an adult demonstration phrase, clearly separate from the
   participant practice material that the lead must approve.
5. Administer the 16 test items. Include one requested repeat, one final-answer
   revision, and one no-response trial after the prescribed prompt.
6. Include a break after ending an attempt. Resume without stopping recording.
7. Finish after the closing, then listen to the audio.

**Trainer observes:** early speech, the prompt, response, intervening speech,
and the break are all audible in one recording. A response marker may exclude
some of that material from ASR; the complete audio must still retain it.

Exercise B: compare delivery modes
----------------------------------

Rehearse each delivery mode that the researcher will actually administer. Use
separate training sessions as needed; creating a session freezes its condition.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 75

   * - Mode
     - Researcher demonstrates
   * - Examiner
     - Cues the item before reading the assigned stimulus, then marks the response after speaking ends.
   * - Browser
     - Uses the provided audio and waits for playback to finish before opening the response window.
   * - M robot
     - Pairs the correct robot, hears actual delivery, and distinguishes a queued request from audible completion.

These are software modes, not names of approved study conditions. The trainer
checks the actual condition sheet and observes M in person before clearing the
researcher for robot administration.

Exercise C: score the final answer
----------------------------------

Use these **invented adult-training examples**, not additional test stimuli.
The quoted target phrases below are not RSR items.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 45 55

   * - Rehearsal example
     - Expected reasoning
   * - The adult gives an inaccurate answer, then revises it to match the target before the attempt ends.
     - Use the final answer. Do not score the earlier response instead.
   * - The adult first matches the target and then changes the final answer to include two scoring errors.
     - Use the final answer: one point, not the earlier two-point answer.
   * - Target: "The bird was not ready." Final answer: "The bird wasn't ready."
     - An appropriate contraction is acceptable; this difference alone is not an error.
   * - The adult repeats a word because of simulated stuttering, with no other differences.
     - Do not count a dysfluency-related repetition as a scoring error.
   * - The reviewed final response has four scoring errors.
     - The manual's explanatory text places four or more errors in the zero-point category. Identify the inconsistent column label and the lead's documented interpretation.

Use the manual for dialect and transposition judgments. Do not teach a general
rule that every word-order change costs the same number of errors, or that a
transcript alone establishes whether a form belongs to a child's language system.

Exercise D: recover without overwriting
---------------------------------------

Use a separate, disposable adult rehearsal session for interruption practice.

* **Marker correction:** finish an attempt with a deliberately imperfect boundary,
  then correct the window with a reason before analysis. Confirm the full audio
  is unchanged.
* **Scoring review:** compare ASR with the audio and apply a reasoned score override.
  Confirm that the original score remains visible.
* **Interrupted capture:** after saving rehearsal data, reload a disposable session
  during recording. Recover the available record using **Review / recover**.
  Identify that capture was interrupted; do not label it continuous or assume
  the last unflushed audio was recovered.
* **Failed service:** if the trainer provides an unavailable scoring/archive service
  scenario, preserve the recording and demonstrate the planned retry or export
  procedure. Do not disrupt a shared live service to stage this exercise.
* **Archive retrieval:** save the rehearsal archive, return to setup, load saved
  archives, and open it under the same researcher login.

Discuss any missed steps, repeat the relevant rehearsal, and proceed to
:doc:`readiness`. A software-only rehearsal is insufficient to verify microphone
placement and audible M playback in the real study setting.
